> How AI is measured on legal work: benchmarks, leaderboards, rubrics, harnesses and datasets, each checked on its own page.

Legal AI is measured in many places and by many methods. This list gathers them for the people who build, buy or study it. Each entry states who made the benchmark and when, what it measures and how large it is, gives the licence where one is stated, and links to the data, the harness, the paper and the leaderboard. Scores and rankings are not reproduced. They date quickly, and the linked pages carry them. Everything here was checked on its own page on 29 October 2026.

## Contract review, drafting and redlining

- [ACORD](https://github.com/The-Atticus-Project/acord) - The Atticus Clause Retrieval Dataset (Wang et al., ACL 2025). It is an expert-annotated retrieval benchmark for contract drafting covering clauses such as limitation of liability, indemnification, change of control and most favoured nation, with 114 queries and over 126,000 query-clause pairs rated 1 to 5. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06582). Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/theatticusproject/acord) (CC BY 4.0). Code under MIT.
- [Benchmarking Humans & AI in Contract Workflows](https://www.legalbenchmarks.ai/research/phase-2-research) - A study by legalbenchmarks.ai (Guo, Souza Rodrigues et al.), September 2025. It is a contract-drafting study comparing AI tools with human lawyers, using a framework that turns lawyers' judgements of draft quality into measurable criteria.
- [Better Call CLAUSE](https://clause-legal.github.io/) - Roy Choudhury et al., Arizona State University (EACL 2026). It is a discrepancy benchmark: over 7,500 perturbed contracts derived from CUAD and ContractNLI with 10 anomaly categories, testing whether models detect embedded flaws and explain their legal significance. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00340). Code on [GitHub](https://github.com/mannanxanand/CLAUSE-Benchmark).
- [ContractEval](https://github.com/olivialiu121/ContractEval) - Liu et al., 2025 (NLLP Workshop 2025). It tests clause-level legal risk identification on the CUAD test set across 41 risk categories, scoring correctness (F1), output effectiveness (Jaccard) and "laziness" (false "no related clause" answers) for 4 proprietary and 15 open-source LLMs. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03080).
- [ContractNLI](https://stanfordnlp.github.io/contract-nli/) - Koreeda and Manning, Stanford (Findings of EMNLP 2021). It frames contract review as document-level natural language inference: 17 fixed hypotheses are classified as entailed, contradicted or not mentioned, with evidence spans, across 607 non-disclosure agreements. Released under CC BY 4.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01799).
- [CUAD](https://github.com/The-Atticus-Project/cuad) - The Contract Understanding Atticus Dataset from The Atticus Project, with Hendrycks et al. (NeurIPS 2021). It is a span-highlighting dataset for legal contract review with over 13,000 expert annotations. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06268). Dataset page at [The Atticus Project](https://www.atticusprojectai.org/cuad); QA version on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/theatticusproject/cuad-qa) (CC BY 4.0).
- [LEDGAR](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.155/) - Tuggener et al., LREC 2020. It is a multi-label corpus of contract provisions taken from SEC filings for large-scale text classification, covering about 100,000 provisions from over 60,000 contracts with over 12,000 labels. A 100-label version is one of the [LexGLUE](https://github.com/coastalcph/lex-glue) tasks.
- [LegalRikai: Open Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11297) - Fujita et al., 2025. It has four complex tasks that emulate Japanese corporate legal practice, built by legal professionals under attorney supervision. Its 100 samples require long-form, structured outputs and are graded by both humans and automated evaluation.
- [MAUD](https://github.com/The-Atticus-Project/maud) - The Merger Agreement Understanding Dataset from The Atticus Project (Wang et al., 2023). It is an expert-annotated reading-comprehension dataset built on the ABA 2021 Public Target Deal Points Study, with over 39,000 examples and over 47,000 annotations. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00876). Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/theatticusproject/maud) (CC BY 4.0).
- [RedlineBench](https://github.com/crosbylegal/redline-bench) - A contract redlining benchmark from Crosby Legal and micro1 (2026). Agents act as in-house counsel at a given turn of a negotiation and must produce a Word .docx with native tracked changes and margin comments, graded against attorney-written rubrics by an LLM judge. It has 140 tasks across 3 SaaS and professional-services MSA scenarios and 4 negotiation turns. Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/crosbylegal/RedlineBench) (CC BY 4.0). Report at [Crosby Intelligence](https://intelligence.crosby.ai/benchmark).

## Legal reasoning, knowledge and exams

- [BigLaw Bench](https://github.com/harveyai/biglaw-bench) - Harvey, 2024. It is a framework of transactional and litigation tasks modelled on billable legal work, scored with custom rubrics for answer quality and source reliability, with Core, Workflows (SPA deal points) and Retrieval suites. The repository holds only sample tasks and rubrics. Full access is by request. Announcement on the [Harvey blog](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/introducing-biglaw-bench).
- [CaseHOLD](https://github.com/reglab/casehold) - Zheng, Guha et al., Stanford RegLab, 2021. It has over 53,000 multiple-choice questions asking for the holding of a cited case. Code under Apache-2.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08671). Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/casehold/casehold).
- [EU-Agent-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21524) - Lichkovski et al., 2025 (NeurIPS 2025). It is a human-curated benchmark that measures whether LLM agents take actions that are illegal under EU law when given benign requests. Agents' function calls are compared against rubrics that cite the relevant legislation, in areas such as data protection, bias/discrimination and scientific integrity.
- [GPT-4 passes the bar exam](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10894685/) - Katz, Bommarito, Gao and Arredondo, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A (2024). It is a zero-shot evaluation of GPT-4 on the full Uniform Bar Exam (MBE, MEE and MPT).
- [Harvey Legal Agent Benchmark (LAB)](https://github.com/harveyai/harvey-labs) - Harvey, 2026. It is an open-source benchmark for LLM agents doing legal work in realistic environments: a task set of instructions, documents and rubrics plus an execution harness (the README badges list 2,010 tasks across 24 practice areas plus contracting). MIT. Announcement and [initial results](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/legal-agent-benchmark-initial-results) on the Harvey blog. Leaderboard at [Vals (HLAB-Vals-1.1)](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/hlab).
- [IL-TUR](https://exploration-lab.github.io/IL-TUR/) - Joshi et al., 2024. It is a benchmark for Indian legal text understanding and reasoning, with tasks in English, Hindi and 9 Indian languages, and it has a public leaderboard. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05399). Code on [GitHub](https://github.com/Exploration-Lab/IL-TUR).
- [J1-ENVS / J1-EVAL (Ready Jurist One)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04037) - Jia et al., 2025. It is an interactive legal environment for LLM agents with six Chinese legal-practice scenarios at three levels of environmental complexity, plus an evaluation framework that scores both task performance and procedural compliance.
- [KBL](https://github.com/lbox-kr/kbl) - Kim et al., LBox (Findings of EMNLP 2024). It is a Korean legal benchmark with 7 legal-knowledge tasks (510 examples), 4 legal-reasoning tasks (288 examples) and the Korean bar exam (2,510 examples), with closed-book and RAG settings. Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lbox/kbl). Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08731). Tasks also ship in lm-evaluation-harness.
- [LAiW](https://github.com/Dai-shen/LAiW) - Dai et al., 2023. It is a Chinese legal LLM benchmark arranged by the logic of legal practice (syllogism) into three levels: basic information retrieval, legal foundation inference and complex legal application. MIT. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05620).
- [LawBench](https://github.com/open-compass/LawBench) - Fei et al., OpenCompass, 2023. It is a Chinese legal benchmark of 20 tasks across three cognitive levels (memorisation, understanding, applying) and five task types. The paper evaluates 51 LLMs. Apache-2.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16289).
- [LegalAgentBench](https://github.com/CSHaitao/LegalAgentBench) - Li et al., 2024. It is an agent benchmark for the Chinese legal domain with 17 corpora, 37 tools and 300 annotated tasks, including multi-hop reasoning and writing, and it scores progress on intermediate steps. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17259).
- [LegalBench](https://github.com/HazyResearch/legalbench) - Guha et al., Stanford / HazyResearch (NeurIPS 2023). It is a collaboratively built legal reasoning benchmark of 162 tasks covering six types of legal reasoning, contributed by 40 contributors. Its contract tasks include the ContractNLI and CUAD families. Each task keeps its source dataset's licence. Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nguha/legalbench). Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11462). Task list on the [project site](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/legalbench/tasks/). Leaderboard at [Vals](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legal_bench).
- [LegalReasonBench](https://www.micro1.ai/benchmark/legal-reason-bench) - micro1, 2026. It tests long-horizon legal reasoning: 50 matters split into 122 assignments, graded against attorney-written rubrics (2,236 criteria) by a three-model LLM judge panel, with each criterion also tagged by IRAC component. A 5-matter sample is on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/micro1-inc/LegalReasonBench) (CC BY 4.0). The rest is held out.
- [LEXam](https://github.com/LEXam-Benchmark/LEXam) - Fan et al., 2025 (ICLR 2026). It has 7,537 questions in English and German taken from 340 law exams across 116 law-school courses, both open-ended and multiple choice. Open answers are graded by an LLM-as-judge ensemble validated by experts. Code under Apache-2.0. Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LEXam-Benchmark/LEXam) (CC BY 4.0). Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12864).
- [LexEval](https://github.com/CSHaitao/LexEval) - Li et al., 2024. It is a Chinese legal benchmark of 23 tasks and 14,150 questions, organised by a taxonomy of legal cognitive abilities and including legal ethics. MIT. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20288).
- [LexGLUE](https://github.com/coastalcph/lex-glue) - Chalkidis et al., 2021. It is a benchmark for English legal language understanding that collects ECtHR (Tasks A and B), SCOTUS, EUR-LEX, LEDGAR, UNFAIR-ToS and CaseHOLD under one evaluation setup. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00976). Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/coastalcph/lex_glue) (CC BY 4.0).
- [LEXTREME](https://github.com/JoelNiklaus/LEXTREME) - Niklaus et al., 2023. It is a multilingual, multi-task legal benchmark of 11 datasets covering 24 languages, with aggregate scores by dataset and by language. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13126). Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/joelniklaus/lextreme) (CC BY 4.0).
- [PLawBench](https://github.com/SKYLENAGE-AI/PLawBench) - Shi et al., 2026. It is a rubric-based benchmark of real-world legal practice with three task categories (public legal consultation, practical case analysis, legal document generation): 850 questions across 13 scenarios and about 12,500 rubric items. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16669).
- [PRBench](https://labs.scale.com/papers/prbench) - Akyürek et al., Scale AI, 2025 (ACL 2026). It has 1,100 expert-authored legal and finance tasks with 19,356 rubric criteria, contributed by 182 professionals and spanning 114 countries and 47 US jurisdictions. Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ScaleAI/PRBench) (CC BY 4.0). Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11562). Legal leaderboard at [Scale Labs](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/prbench-legal).
- [Re-evaluating GPT-4's bar exam performance](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/153986) - Eric Martínez, MIT, Artificial Intelligence and Law (2024). It re-examines the percentile estimates and essay grading behind earlier reports of GPT-4's Uniform Bar Exam result.
- [SCALE: One Law, Many Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09237) - Stern et al., 2023. It is a benchmark built on the Swiss legal system that tests long documents (up to 50K tokens), five languages, retrieval, court view generation, summarisation, citation extraction and eight classification tasks. Resources are released under CC BY-SA.

## Retrieval, RAG and citations

- [Bar Exam QA and Housing Statute QA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03970) - Zheng et al., Stanford RegLab, 2025. These are two legal RAG benchmarks that require reasoning-heavy retrieval: Multistate Bar Exam questions paired with supporting passages, and yes/no housing-law questions across US jurisdictions paired with the statutes that answer them. Bar Exam QA data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/reglab/barexam_qa) (CC BY-SA 4.0).
- [CLERC](https://github.com/abehou/CLERC) - Hou et al., Johns Hopkins (NAACL 2025). It is a case-law retrieval and retrieval-augmented analysis-generation dataset built from the Caselaw Access Project, with tasks to find the citations for a piece of legal analysis and to write the analysis. Data on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/jhu-clsp/CLERC). Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17186).
- [COLIEE](https://coliee.org/COLIEE2026/overview) - The Competition on Legal Information Extraction and Entailment, an annual shared task held with ICAIL; 2026 was its 13th edition. The tasks cover case-law retrieval and entailment, Japanese Civil Code statute retrieval and entailment, and a pilot task on Japanese tort judgment prediction. Datasets are distributed through the competition site.
- [Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools](https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/) - Magesh, Surani, Dahl, Suzgun, Manning and Ho, Stanford RegLab and HAI, 2024. It is the first preregistered empirical evaluation of RAG-based commercial legal research tools, using over 200 open-ended legal queries and a typology that separates incorrect answers from misgrounded ones. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362). Summary at [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-or-more-benchmarking-queries).
- [Large Legal Fictions](https://github.com/reglab/legal_hallucinations) - Dahl, Magesh, Suzgun and Ho, Journal of Legal Analysis (2024). It profiles legal hallucinations in general-purpose LLMs across jurisdictions, courts, time periods and cases, and offers a typology of legal hallucinations. The repository holds the replication code. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01301).
- [Legal RAG Bench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/isaacus/legal-rag-bench) - Butler and Butler, Isaacus, 2026. It is an end-to-end legal RAG benchmark: 4,876 passages from the Victorian Criminal Charge Book and 100 hand-written expert questions with long-form answers, evaluated with a full factorial design that separates retrieval errors from reasoning errors. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01710).
- [LegalBench-RAG](https://github.com/zeroentropy-ai/legalbenchrag) - Pipitone et al., ZeroEntropy, 2024. It is a retrieval benchmark built by tracing LegalBench queries back to their source passages: 6,858 query-answer pairs over a corpus of more than 79M characters, annotated by legal experts, plus a "mini" version. MIT. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10343).
- [LePaRD](https://github.com/rmahari/LePaRD) - Mahari et al., 2023. It is a large collection of US federal judicial citations to precedent in context, used for legal passage retrieval (predicting the precedent passage a judge will cite). Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09356).
- [LexAgentHallu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09754) - Zhou et al., 2026. It is a benchmark for hallucinations in tool-using legal agents across multi-step trajectories: 3,414 instances across 17 legal categories and 6 task types, annotated with a two-layer hallucination taxonomy.
- [Massive Legal Embedding Benchmark (MLEB)](https://isaacus.com/mleb) - Butler et al., Isaacus, 2025. It is an embedding and retrieval benchmark of ten expert-annotated datasets (seven new) covering the US, UK, EU, Australia, Ireland and Singapore, across cases, legislation, regulatory guidance, contracts and literature. Code and results on [GitHub](https://github.com/isaacus-dev/mleb) (MIT). Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19365).

## Leaderboards and third-party evaluations

- [Artificial Lawyer: GenAI Benchmarking](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/category/genai-benchmarking/) - The trade publication's running category of coverage on legal AI benchmarking studies and initiatives.
- [DELTA](https://www.legalbenchmarks.ai/research/delta-dutch-legal-research-benchmark) - A benchmark from legalbenchmarks.ai with Zeno as research partner, September 2026. It is a Dutch legal research benchmark built from more than 200 Dutch practice research assignments, reviewed by 12 partner firms and graded on substance, form and citation.
- [Legal Benchmarks leaderboard](https://www.legalbenchmarks.ai/leaderboard) - A leaderboard from legalbenchmarks.ai, run by Anna Guo and collaborators. It is a model leaderboard for contract workflows and data extraction that scores substance (task pass across two independent attempts) and form. Earlier reports include [Putting AI to the Test in Real-World Legal Work](https://www.legalbenchmarks.ai/research/phase-1-research) (April 2025, data extraction, 6 tools, 108 outputs scored by experts).
- [LinksAI English law benchmark](https://linklaters.com/insights/blogs/digilinks/2025/february/uk-the-linksai-english-law-benchmark-version-2) - Linklaters. It has 50 English-law questions across 10 practice areas, each marked out of 10 by senior lawyers (5 for substance, 3 for citations, 2 for clarity). Version 1 was published in October 2023 and version 2 on 10 February 2025.
- [prinzbench](https://github.com/prinz-ai/prinzbench) - A private benchmark created in January 2026 that ranks LLMs on legal research and analysis (75 points) and on locating obscure public information online (24 points). The repository publishes the leaderboard and methodology notes. The questions are not released.
- [Thomson Reuters CoCoBench](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/why-legal-ai-needs-a-new-standard-inside-thomson-reuters-cocobench/) - Thomson Reuters, May 2026. It is the published description of an internal benchmark with hundreds of attorney-written tasks (research, drafting, review, multi-step reasoning), each with a gold-standard response, graded by attorneys on both the deliverable and its citation record. The dataset is not public. Earlier methodology posts: [Legal AI Benchmarking: CoCounsel](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/legal-ai-benchmarking-cocounsel/) (October 2024) and [Benchmarking CoCounsel with Scorecard](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/from-testcase-to-trust-benchmarking-cocounsel-with-scorecard/) (September 2025).
- [Vals AI legal benchmarks](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks) - Vals AI runs public model leaderboards that are regularly updated. The open ones are [LegalBench](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legal_bench) and [HLAB-Vals-1.1](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/hlab), an agentic legal-work benchmark run with Harvey. The proprietary ones are the [Vals Legal Index](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legal_index) (research and drafting weighted by share of US law-office revenue), [Legal Research Bench](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legal_research), [Immigration Bench](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/immigration) and the archived [CaseLaw v2](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/case_law_v2) (private Q&A over Canadian court cases).
- [Vals Legal AI Report (VLAIR)](https://www.vals.ai/industry-reports/vlair) - Vals AI, February 2025. It is an evaluation of legal AI products on seven tasks (data extraction, document Q&A, summarisation, redlining, transcript analysis, chronology generation, EDGAR research) against a lawyer control group. A follow-up, [VLAIR - Legal Research](https://www.vals.ai/industry-reports/vlair-10-14-25) (October 2025), covers 200 US legal research questions scored on accuracy, authoritativeness and appropriateness.

## Frameworks, rubrics and harnesses

- [HELM legal scenarios](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm/tree/main/src/helm/benchmark/scenarios) - Stanford CRFM. The HELM framework includes scenarios for LegalBench, LexGLUE, LEXTREME, CaseHOLD, legal summarisation, legal contract summarisation and Arabic legal tasks. Apache-2.0.
- [Legal AI Evaluation Framework](https://www.legalbenchmarks.ai/research/evaluation-framework) - Guo, Weijtmans, Schrijvers and Kim, legalbenchmarks.ai, February 2026. It is an open-access procurement framework with 8 core criteria (strategic fit, functionality, robustness, security, data privacy, vendor risk, adoption support, cost) and 100 sub-tests, used across three stages (pre-demo checklist, demo scorecard, pilot scorecard).
- [Litig AI Benchmark Initiative](https://www.litig.org/ai/introduction) - The Legal IT Innovators Group (UK), from 2024. It is an industry initiative that publishes an [AI Transparency Charter](https://www.litig.org/ai/transparency-charter), a product transparency statement template, a use-case framework and a glossary of AI testing and benchmarking terms.
- [lm-evaluation-harness: LegalBench and KBL tasks](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/tree/main/lm_eval/tasks/legalbench) - EleutherAI. The `legalbench` group implements the five-task LegalBench subset used by HELM plus the full 14-task Contract NLI suite, loaded from the `nguha/legalbench` dataset. A separate [`kbl`](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/tree/main/lm_eval/tasks/kbl) task covers the Korean legal benchmark. MIT.
- [LRAGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01840) - Park et al., 2025. It is an open-source tool for evaluating legal RAG systems that varies the retrieval corpus, retriever, reranker, LLM backbone and metric, and was validated on KBL, LegalBench and LawBench. Code on [GitHub](https://github.com/hoorangyee/LRAGE).

## Datasets and corpora

- [Cambridge Law Corpus](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/research/srg/projects/law) - Östling et al., University of Cambridge (NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks). It contains over 250,000 UK court cases, with expert case-outcome annotations for 638 cases. Access is restricted to research use. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12269).
- [Caselaw Access Project](https://lil.law.harvard.edu/our-work/caselaw-access-project/) - Harvard Law School Library Innovation Lab. It holds over 6.7 million US state and federal court decisions covering 360 years, digitised from 40 million pages between 2013 and 2018, and served at [case.law](https://case.law/). Copies on Hugging Face from [Free Law](https://huggingface.co/datasets/free-law/Caselaw_Access_Project) (CC0, gated) and [Common Pile](https://huggingface.co/datasets/common-pile/caselaw_access_project).
- [EDGAR-CORPUS](https://huggingface.co/datasets/eloukas/edgar-corpus) - Loukas et al., 2021. It contains annual reports (10-K) of US public companies spanning more than 25 years, split into items. Apache-2.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14394). Crawler on [GitHub](https://github.com/lefterisloukas/edgar-crawler).
- [Find Case Law](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/) - The National Archives (UK). It is the official service publishing judgments and tribunal decisions from courts in England and Wales and UK-wide courts and tribunals, with its own reuse and licensing terms.
- [ILDC](https://github.com/Exploration-Lab/CJPE) - Malik et al., 2021. The Indian Legal Documents Corpus has about 35,000 Indian Supreme Court cases annotated with decisions, plus an expert-annotated explanation test set, for court judgment prediction and explanation. Code under GPL-3.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13562).
- [LawInstruct](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lawinstruct/lawinstruct) - Niklaus et al., 2024. It is an instruction-tuning resource that aggregates 58 annotated legal datasets across 17 jurisdictions and 24 languages, about 12M examples. MIT on the dataset card. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02127).
- [MultiEURLEX](https://huggingface.co/datasets/coastalcph/multi_eurlex) - Chalkidis et al., 2021. It has 65,000 EU laws in 23 official languages, labelled with EUROVOC concepts, for multilingual and cross-lingual legal classification. CC BY 4.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00904). An earlier English-only set, EURLEX57K, is described in [Chalkidis et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02192).
- [MultiLegalPile](https://huggingface.co/datasets/joelniklaus/Multi_Legal_Pile) - Niklaus et al., 2023. It is a 689GB legal corpus in 24 languages from 17 jurisdictions, with licences that vary by source. The dataset card lists CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02069).
- [Open Australian Legal Corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/isaacus/open-australian-legal-corpus) - Isaacus. It is a multijurisdictional open corpus of Australian legislation, bills and court and tribunal decisions: 229,122 texts and about 1.4 billion tokens. Builder code on [GitHub](https://github.com/isaacus-dev/open-australian-legal-corpus-creator).
- [Pile of Law](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pile-of-law/pile-of-law) - Henderson et al., Stanford, 2022. It is a 256GB English-language corpus of legal and administrative text (court opinions, contracts, administrative rules, legislative records). CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00220).

## Surveys and papers

- [Evaluation of Large Language Models in Legal Applications: Challenges, Methods, and Future Directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15267) - Hu et al., 2026. It is a survey of how LLMs are evaluated on legal tasks, covering outcome correctness, reasoning reliability and trustworthiness, and it classifies benchmarks by task design, data and metrics.
- [Large Language Models in Law: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03718) - Lai et al., 2023. It surveys legal LLMs, their uses in judicial settings and their limitations in data, algorithms and judicial practice.
- [Large Language Models Meet Legal Artificial Intelligence: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09969) - Hou et al., 2025. It reviews 16 legal LLM series, 47 LLM-based frameworks, 15 benchmarks and 29 datasets. Resources on [GitHub](https://github.com/ZhitianHou/LLMs4LegalAI).
- [Legal Reasoning Is Not Lawyering: Rethinking Legal Benchmarks for Pro Se Access to Justice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23716) - Lou et al., 2026 (ICML 2026). It is a position paper arguing that current benchmarks measure reasoning over inputs already cleaned up by experts and that access-to-justice claims need testing on unrepresented litigants' noisy inputs.
- [Natural Language Processing in the Legal Domain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12039) - Hartung, Katz et al., 2023. It is a bibliometric survey of nearly one thousand NLP and law papers published between 2013 and 2024, tracking tasks, languages, methods and reproducibility.
- [There is no free benchmark: An institutional view of legal AI benchmarking](https://dho.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/PNAS_Bench.pdf) - Guha, Zhang, Tsang, Manning, Nyarko and Ho, PNAS (July 2026). It is a perspective arguing for public legal AI benchmarking and setting out the institutional design choices (why, who, what, how) and how benchmarks can be captured or watered down. DOI [10.1073/pnas.2509757122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2509757122).
